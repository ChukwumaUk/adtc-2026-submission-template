Released improved cassava varieties in Nigeria (BASICS-II)

The eleven promoted varieties are: Ayaya, Farmer's Pride, Fine Face, Sunshine, TME419, Dixon, Game Changer, Obasanjo-2, Hope, Poundable and Baba-70. All have yield potential above 30 tonnes per hectare.

Variety names and their official released codes:
Ayaya (also called Beautiful) has the official name CR36-5.
Farmer's Pride has the official name IBA961632.
Fine Face has the official name IBA980505.
Sunshine has the official name IBA070593.
TME419 has the official name TME419, also released as TMEB419.
Dixon has the official name IBA980581, also written TMS-IBA980581.
Game Changer has the official name TMS13F1160P0004.
Obasanjo-2 has the official name TMS13F1343P0022.
Hope has the official name NR130124.
Poundable has the official name TMEB693.
Baba-70 has the official name TMS-IBA000070.

TME419 (Released name: TMEB419)
This is a popularly known and widely accepted name adapted from its originally released code name. Processors love this variety for its stem properties. Its straight and erect stem allows for ease of mechanization. Also endearing are its high yield and stability of yield as well as low rate of physiological deterioration.
TME419 Yield: 36t/ha
TME419 Dry matter content: 40%
TME419 Physiology: Erect, straight type excellent for mechanization
TME419 Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
TME419 Agro ecology best suited in Nigeria: Suitable for all Nigeria ecology
TME419 Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
TME419 Other features: Green petiole, light brown stem, light/cream root, CMD resistance (cassava mosaic disease resistance), low cyanide potential. Good for starch, flour, garri, and fresh consumption

Dixon (Released name: TMS-IBA980581)
The variety Dixon is named after the renowned breeder, Dr Dixon, in honour of his landmark achievements in cassava breeding thus contributing to eradicating hunger and poverty in sub-Saharan Africa. Over the years he has bred many cassava varieties, however this is his favourite because of its red petiole colour and resilience.
Dixon Yield: 35t/ha
Dixon Dry matter content: 35%
Dixon Physiology: Erect plant type, excellent drought tolerance
Dixon Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Dixon Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Dixon Other features: Red petiole, silver-green stem, white root, resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD) and cassava green mite (CGM), drought tolerant. Good for garri production

Ayaya (Released name: CR36-5)
Ayaya means beautiful in Ibibio, one of Nigeria's several dialects. It is so named because of its beautiful and attractive plant type. Large scale processors like this variety for flour, gari, fufu and pupuru.
Ayaya Yield: 35t/ha
Ayaya Dry matter content: 40%
Ayaya Physiology: Erect growth habit
Ayaya Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Ayaya Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Ayaya Other features: Purple petiole, light brown stem, white root, drought tolerant, tolerant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), multiple pest resistance, excellent for flour and starch production, stable dry matter

Sunshine (Released name: TMS-IBA070593)
The sun is bright and yellow, so is this variety. It is named Sunshine for its particularly bright yellow colour. Farmers believe that the variety is good for young and elderly people alike who love eating garri but possess certain medical conditions that constrain them from eating it. This is because this particular variety is packed with Vitamin A which is very healthy for eyesight and other bodily functions.
Sunshine Yield: 30t/ha
Sunshine Dry matter content: 30%
Sunshine Physiology: Compact branch type
Sunshine Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Sunshine Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Sunshine Other features: Purple green petiole, brown stem, yellow root, drought tolerant, multiple pest resistance, high carotenoid content, excellent for yellow garri and other biofortified food products

Fineface (Released name IBA980505)
Fineface is known for its exceptionally attractive garri appearance. Its garri looks very white and fine. It has longer post harvest deterioration when compared to other varieties.
Fineface Yield: 34t/ha
Fineface Dry matter content: 35%
Fineface Physiology: Attractive umbrella shaped plant type good for weed control
Fineface Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Fineface Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Fineface Other features: Green purple petiole, silver green stem, white root, resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), excellent for garri production

Game Changer (Released name TMS13F1160P0004)
The name Game Changer derives from the massive yield of this variety at harvest, as farmers believe that cultivating this variety will change their livelihood status by eradicating hunger and poverty because of its yield and food quality. Its leaves are very attractive to sight. It has good root shape and low water content.
Game Changer Yield: 38t/ha
Game Changer Dry matter content: 44%
Game Changer Physiology: Compact branch type
Game Changer Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Game Changer Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Game Changer Other features: Light brown stem, white root, thick peels preferred for feedmeals, multiple pests and disease resistance, excellent for starch and flour production, stable dry matter

Poundable (Released name TMEB693)
Poundable derives its name from the poundability of this cassava variety. Farmers often compare it to yam because of its mealy nature. It can be used to make any food products due to its poundability. Farmers often consume this variety boiled or roasted while on the field before the arrival of other meals.
Poundable Yield: 32t/ha
Poundable Dry matter content: 38%
Poundable Physiology: Attractive umbrella
Poundable Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Poundable Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Poundable Other features: Dark brown stem, white root, moderate resistance to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), low cyanide potential, highly poundable, best for fresh consumption, highly mealy

Farmer's Pride (Released name TMS-IBA961632)
Farmer's Pride is so called because farmers are proud of the stability and finesse of this variety. The leaves are very attractive at the early stage and farmers love the height of the variety because it is average, thereby preventing severe lodging as compared to others.
Farmer's Pride Yield: 35 t/ha
Farmer's Pride Dry matter content: 39%
Farmer's Pride Physiology: Erect plant type
Farmer's Pride Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Farmer's Pride Type of soil best grown on: Sandy-loam
Farmer's Pride Other features: Green purple petiole, light brown stem, white root, drought tolerant, resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), good garri quality, good for starch and flour production, stable dry matter

Baba-70 (Released name TMS-IBA000070)
The variety is good for gari and fufu.
Baba-70 Yield: 37.5 t/ha
Baba-70 Dry matter content: 38.5%, starch 28.0%
Baba-70 Physiology: Compact plant type, light brown stem, and red petiole colour
Baba-70 Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Baba-70 Agro ecology best suited in Nigeria: Rain Forest and Southern Guinea Savanna
Baba-70 Type of soil best grown on: Loam and sandy loam
Baba-70 Other features: High yielding and early bulking, good weed control, moderate dry matter content, resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), excellent for gari and fufu

Obasanjo-2 (Released name TMS13F1343P0022)
This variety is named in honour of Nigeria's former President, Chief Olusegun Obasanjo, who is a cassava farmer. During his reign Chief Obasanjo championed the transformation of cassava with emphasis on its commercialization and industrialization. Consequently cassava production rose from 33 million tons in 1999 to 43 million tons. Obasanjo-2 addresses the needs of the commercial sector with its excellent qualities for flour and starch.
Obasanjo-2 Yield: 38.7 t/ha
Obasanjo-2 Dry matter content: 40.7%, starch 28.6%
Obasanjo-2 Physiology: Good umbrella plant type, greenish purple petiole, silver brown stems. Morphology offers good weed control.
Obasanjo-2 Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Obasanjo-2 Agro ecology best suited in Nigeria: Rain Forest and Southern Guinea Savanna
Obasanjo-2 Type of soil best grown on: Loam and sandy loam
Obasanjo-2 Other features: Resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), cassava anthracnose disease (CAD), cassava mealybug (CM), cassava bacterial blight (CBB) and cassava green mite (CGM). High starch, high dry matter content and high fresh root yield, good for flour and starch production.

Hope (Released name NR130124)
This variety is good for gari and fufu, both products highly consumed in Nigeria. Farmers who grow the variety have the hope of food security.
Hope Yield: 40.1 t/ha
Hope Dry matter content: 33.2%, starch 24.3%
Hope Physiology: Compact branching pattern
Hope Maturity period: 10 - 12 months
Hope Agro ecology best suited in Nigeria: Rain Forest and Southern Guinea Savanna
Hope Type of soil best grown on: Loam to sandy loam
Hope Other features: Moderate dry matter, resistant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD), cassava anthracnose disease (CAD), cassava mealybug (CM), cassava bacterial blight (CBB) and cassava green mite (CGM). Good for mechanization and has high fresh root yield. Excellent for gari and fufu production.

Disease and pest resistance of improved cassava varieties
Varieties resistant or tolerant to cassava mosaic disease (CMD): TME419, Dixon, Ayaya, Fineface, Farmer's Pride, Baba-70, Obasanjo-2, Hope, and Poundable (moderate resistance).
Varieties resistant to cassava green mite (CGM): Dixon, Obasanjo-2, Hope.
Varieties resistant to cassava mealybug (CM): Obasanjo-2, Hope.
Varieties resistant to cassava bacterial blight (CBB): Obasanjo-2, Hope.
Varieties resistant to cassava anthracnose disease (CAD): Obasanjo-2, Hope.
Varieties with multiple pest resistance: Ayaya, Sunshine, Game Changer, Obasanjo-2, Hope.
Drought tolerant varieties: Dixon, Ayaya, Sunshine, Farmer's Pride.
The improved cassava variety that gives the highest yield is Hope, which produces 40.1 tonnes of fresh roots per hectare. The second highest yielding variety is Obasanjo-2 at 38.7 tonnes per hectare, followed by Game Changer at 38 tonnes per hectare and Baba-70 at 37.5 tonnes per hectare. A farmer who wants the best yield from an improved variety should consider Hope, Obasanjo-2 or Game Changer.

Ranked yields of the improved cassava varieties: Hope 40.1 tonnes per hectare, Obasanjo-2 38.7 t/ha, Game Changer 38 t/ha, Baba-70 37.5 t/ha, TME419 36 t/ha, Ayaya 35 t/ha, Dixon 35 t/ha, Farmer's Pride 35 t/ha, Fineface 34 t/ha, Poundable 32 t/ha, Sunshine 30 t/ha.
Obasanjo-2 and Hope have the broadest resistance, being resistant to cassava mosaic disease, cassava anthracnose disease, cassava mealybug, cassava bacterial blight and cassava green mite.


Choosing a cassava variety for a specific need

Which cassava variety has the highest starch and dry matter content? Game Changer has the highest dry matter content at 44 percent. Obasanjo-2 follows at 40.7 percent dry matter with 28.6 percent starch, then TME419 and Ayaya at 40 percent dry matter each. Baba-70 has 38.5 percent dry matter with 28 percent starch. A farmer growing cassava for starch, flour or ethanol should choose Game Changer, Obasanjo-2, TME419 or Ayaya, because higher dry matter means more starch in the roots.

Which cassava variety helps control weeds? Fineface has an attractive umbrella shaped plant type that is good for weed control. Obasanjo-2 has a good umbrella plant type whose morphology offers good weed control. Baba-70 gives good weed control and is early bulking. Poundable also has an umbrella plant type. Varieties that form a closing canopy shade the ground and suppress weed growth, which reduces the need for weeding and herbicide.

Which cassava variety is best for drought or dry conditions? Dixon has excellent drought tolerance. Ayaya, Sunshine and Farmer's Pride are also drought tolerant. A farmer in a dry area or facing an unreliable rainy season should choose Dixon first.

Which cassava variety is best for making garri? Fineface is the best variety for making garri, because its garri looks very white and fine and it is known for its exceptionally attractive garri appearance. Fineface produces garri that looks very white and fine, and is known for its attractive garri appearance. Farmer's Pride has good garri quality. Dixon is good for garri production. TME419, Baba-70 and Hope are all good for garri. Sunshine produces yellow garri and is rich in Vitamin A.

Which cassava variety is best for fufu, pounding or fresh eating? Poundable is highly poundable and mealy, and is best for fresh consumption, often eaten boiled or roasted. Baba-70 and Hope are excellent for fufu. Ayaya is liked for fufu and pupuru.

Which cassava variety is best for flour and starch production? Ayaya is excellent for flour and starch production. Game Changer is excellent for starch and flour production. Obasanjo-2 is good for flour and starch and addresses the needs of the commercial sector. TME419 is good for starch and flour. Farmer's Pride is good for starch and flour production.

Which cassava variety is best for mechanized farming? TME419 has an erect straight stem that is excellent for mechanization. Hope is also good for mechanization.

Which cassava variety is rich in Vitamin A? Sunshine has a yellow root, high carotenoid content, and is packed with Vitamin A. It is excellent for yellow garri and other biofortified food products.

Which cassava variety matures fastest? All eleven improved varieties mature in 10 to 12 months.

What soil do improved cassava varieties grow best on? Most of these varieties grow best on sandy-loam soil. Baba-70, Obasanjo-2 and Hope grow well on loam and sandy loam. Baba-70, Obasanjo-2 and Hope are best suited to the Rain Forest and Southern Guinea Savanna zones of Nigeria. TME419 is suitable for all Nigerian ecologies.

Which cassava variety is best for a commercial or industrial farm? A commercial operation producing starch, flour or ethanol should consider Obasanjo-2, which combines a high yield of 38.7 tonnes per hectare with 40.7 percent dry matter, 28.6 percent starch, resistance to all five major cassava pests and diseases, and good weed control. Game Changer is another strong choice with 38 tonnes per hectare and the highest dry matter at 44 percent.


How can I increase the starch content of my cassava? The starch in cassava roots comes from dry matter, so the main way a farmer increases starch content is by planting a variety with high dry matter content. Game Changer has the highest dry matter at 44 percent. Obasanjo-2 has 40.7 percent dry matter and 28.6 percent starch. TME419 and Ayaya both have 40 percent dry matter. Baba-70 has 38.5 percent dry matter and 28 percent starch. Harvesting at full maturity, 10 to 12 months after planting, also allows the roots to accumulate the most starch. Harvesting too early gives lower starch content.

How can I get more starch from my cassava harvest? Plant a high dry matter variety such as Game Changer, Obasanjo-2, TME419 or Ayaya, and harvest at 10 to 12 months when the roots have accumulated the most starch.
